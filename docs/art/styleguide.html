Bobbleheads: world art style guide

The visual language for everything in Star Rebellion that isn't HUD: characters, kit, terrain, effects, ships, Haven Rock and the galaxy map. It sits alongside the HUD style guide, uses the same ink and faction colours, and is built the same way.

Every picture here is drawn live by sr-art.js, the module Claude Code will add to the game. What you see on this page is exactly what the game will draw.

Rules of the house

Faces carry state

Aiming, shaken, hacking or down should read off the face before any number. Every pose picks an expression.

Red is ours, blue is theirs

Every rebel wears a red scarf or armband. Every hostile has a blue visor, scarf, hatband or trim. Gold stays with the HUD.

Swap, don't redraw

A character is assembled each frame from slots: body, head, weapon, gear and pose. New kit is a data entry, never new art.

One ink, one light

Ink outlines everywhere: 2.6 on bodies, 2.2 on held things, 3 on terrain. Light comes from the top left, with one cel-shade tone.

Rebels make do

Rebel kit and ships are patched, faded, taped and personal. The Hegemony's are clean, white and navy, glowing and faceless.

A quiet world, loud people

Terrain is low-contrast and muted. Saturation is spent on characters, props you can use, and effects.

Palette

Faction colours come straight from the HUD kit (SR_THEME.C), so changing --sr-rebel recolours scarves, stripes and HUD together. The world palette lives in SR_ART.W and SR_ART.BIOME.

Shade is always the base colour darkened by 22% (shade(col,-.22)). Highlights are white at 25–55% opacity. Never shade with black at low opacity, because it muddies on dark terrain.

Construction

At scale 1, one unit is one CSS pixel at zoom 1, which makes a Bobblehead about the width of the old token. The anchor is the point between the feet: that's where the unit's map position, depth sort, selection ring and shadow all sit. Characters are drawn in back-to-front layers: feet, slung weapon, pack, body, vest, belt items, scarf, shoulder weapon, head, face, hair, helmet, then held weapon, hands and props.

Faces

Nine expressions cover every state. Pupils look toward the aim direction. The brows do most of the work, so keep them thick. Blinks happen every few seconds when idle or moving. Hegemony troopers never show a face: their two visor slits narrow, tilt or go round instead. Policebots show a screen, and because robots don't panic, they show ?! rather than sweat.

Cast and archetypes

Named crew have fixed genes in SR_ART.CAST, but their injuries, traits, rank and kit still show, like everyone else's. Every other rebel is generated (next section). Hostiles and bystanders come from an archetype in SR_ART.ARCH, chosen from the unit's existing flags.

Portraits

SR_ART.portrait() draws head and shoulders in a circle, for crew lists, recruit cards and the comms log. These replace the current initials.

Generated rebels

Most rebels aren't authored. They're generated by the rebel system (Rebel.gen), then shaped by everything that happens to them. SR_ART.lookOf(p) turns any rebel into a character. Nothing about their look is saved. Their face comes from a hash of p.id, so a rebel looks the same in every session, and everything else is read live from the rebel. Old saves need no migration.

A rebel's life shows

The same rebel across a career: recruited, blooded and promoted, injured, scarred, fitted with a prosthetic, decorated, commissioned, and finally made a Hero. Each stage is just a change to their record.

Armoury: Revolution Level 1

This is the Rev Level 1 kit from Gadgets, Gear and Gunships. Every item has two drawings from the same code: item art for the Arsenal, the Black Market and loot (SR_ART.item()), and the item in use on a character. Crafted and scavenged kit looks it, with tape, pipes and exposed cores.

Manufacturers

The doc now lists fifteen manufacturers (TenTiU is gone). Each has a badge (SR_ART.makerBadge()) and a look, so players can tell a maker's kit at a glance.

Five of the makers have no items in the docs yet, so each gets one style mock to set its look. They're marked mock:1 in the kit and stay out of the game data until real items exist. Helix now makes the game's medical items: the Medpack and Stim are clinical white and teal, with rounded cases and the double-helix mark. The LMC Arclight is a white ceramic laser pistol with a violet energy cell and status LEDs. It fires a violet laser beam, which is now the colour for laser damage. The Nomad Drifter Coat is a canvas duster with leather straps, a patched shoulder plate and visible stitching. General Astronautics builds the Hegemony's capital ships. The Bulwark-class Frigate mock sets the look: slab grey hull, navy spine and bands, a stencilled hull number, a faceless bridge slit, banks of engines, and turrets and missile racks on mounts, using the same loadout system as the fighters. Armour and headgear

There are four new head items, so every head-slot item now has art. The Frontier Hardhat (now Praxon) is still the Soldier default. The Police Helmet's tinted visor covers the eyes, which keeps security forces half-faceless. The Auto Head-Helm is a dead Auto's head, worn with its screen on the brow. The Cowboy Hat and Baseball Cap are cosmetic. All of them come from the game's p.gear.head, using the same keys.

Equipment slots and the back slot

A rebel's type decides what they carry. Soldiers and Marines get a primary, a secondary, a back item and three gadgets. Pilots get a secondary and three gadgets. Support carry nothing. The back slot shows on the character: Heavy weapons and deployables are strapped across the back (p.gear.back), and you can see them from the front, side and back.

Deployables

A deployable is carried on the back and set up with an action (the deploy pose). The Razorrat LMG goes onto a tripod with its gunner crouched behind it (SR_ART.deployable()). The Riot Shield comes off the back and into the hands, replacing the primary. The Mining Laser sweeps its cone, then needs the Recharge pose.

Gadgets

Each gadget has item art, a spot on the belt while it's carried, and a pose while it's used. BLAMCo kit wears the red-and-yellow branding. The Guardian Angel Drone hovers at its owner's shoulder while it's active.

Weapon traits and fire modes

Damage types and traits each have an icon (SR_ART.icon(ctx, key, x, y, size)) for the Arsenal, Black Market cards and the engagement panel. Fire modes are gold, because they're something you choose. Other traits are white. Damage types use their own colour.

The fire-mode toggle

A weapon with more than one fire mode gets a segmented toggle in the attack window, built in the HUD kit's chunky button style. The selected mode is gold and sits pressed in, and F cycles through the modes. On the battlefield, the mode changes how the shot looks. Automatic fires a flickering burst with ejecting brass and a shaking recoil. Semi-auto gives steady shots. Single shot fires one big flash. Fan hammer has the off hand slapping the hammer through three quick flashes.

Traits on the battlefield

Armour, shields and health

The Ground Combat doc puts armour and shields over the health bar wherever health appears. SR_ART.vitals() draws all three as layered segments. Health is the familiar row of pips, going green, then orange, then red. Armour sits on top as slanted steel plates, which crack when emptied. Shield sits over both as glowing cyan hexes in a band. Each layer is a different shape as well as a different colour, so they stay readable on a phone and for colour-blind players. While a shield is up, the character also has a faint hex bubble that flares when it's hit. Armour hits throw sparks and a ricochet streak.

Juice

Juice is the feedback that makes every action feel like it landed: anticipation, impact and follow-through, built from several small cues stacked together. The kit had effects, but they played flat and alone. This pass reworks them around a few rules.

Every action answers back

A shot, a hit, a door, a button: each gets at least three cues at once, such as motion, a flash, particles, a number and sound.

Impact freezes, then shakes

A big hit freezes the game for 40–160 ms (hitstop), then shakes the screen in proportion to the hit. The target flashes white and squashes.

Nothing moves linearly

Pops overshoot (ease.outBack), falls bounce (outBounce), panels ease in and out. Linear movement reads as broken.

Leave a mark

Follow-through lingers. Casings bounce, smoke rises, scorch marks stay, debris settles. The battlefield remembers the fight.

Big things are big

Shake, freeze and particles scale with the event. A pistol barely nudges the screen; a rocket rocks it. Small hits stay small, so big ones still feel big.

Respect the player

There's a Screen shake setting. Reduced motion turns off shake, hitstop and drifting particles. Nothing flashes more than three times a second.

Shots: set by the maker, patterned by the fire mode

No two weapons shoot alike. The maker sets the palette, the same for both factions: Bhord tracers are warm yellow, Devlin & Son white-gold, Patriot crisp white-blue, Fightstar hot orange, LMC violet, Praxon industrial red-orange, AutoCom cyan, and crafted kit unstable magenta. The weapon sets the shape: tracers, a heavy slug with a smoke trail, a near-instant rail line for the Longhorn, a pellet cone, needle darts, plasma bolts, wobbling unstable bolts, ring pulses, laser beams and smoky rockets. The fire mode sets the pattern: automatic is a staggered, spreading burst, fan hammer is three rounds in quick succession, and single shot is one round with a bigger flash. Muzzle flashes use the same palette and shape (muzzleFor()).

An attack is an attempt, not a bullet

In the rules, an attack is one roll: it hits for its damage or it misses. On screen, an attack is a burst of fire, re-rolled every time so no two look alike (SR_ART.volley()). Single shot fires exactly one round. Semi-auto fires 3 to 5 rounds, with 1 to 3 landing. Automatic fires 6 to 10, with roughly half landing. Fan hammer fires 3, with 2 or 3 landing. Shotguns fire 2 or 3 blasts, and lasers 2 or 3 pulses. The damage is split across the rounds that land, so a 10-damage hit might read 3, miss, 3, 4, and the total always matches the rules. A missed attack sprays every round past the target into the ground behind. In the gallery above, every row re-rolls each loop, and the numbers on the right show that loop's split; every third loop is a miss. This is purely visual.

Impacts: what was hit, and with what

SR_ART.impact() depends on the surface and the damage type. Flesh bleeds: bullets throw a spray of blood droplets along the shot, plasma and lasers sear with a glowing ring and a wisp of smoke, and melee lands with impact lines. Flesh hits leave a blood splat on the ground (bloodSplat()). Armour throws sparks, a ricochet streak and a metallic ring. Shields ripple in hexes. Cover puffs dust and chips. Robots spark, arc and leak black oil. There's a Blood setting (SR_ART.setGore(false)) that swaps blood for dust and impact lines, for players or platforms that need it.

Try it: ground

Pick a weapon, a fire mode, a result and a target, then fire. Each attack is a fresh volley: one roll, with the damage split across the rounds that land. The riot rifleman's armour sparks until it's gone, then he bleeds. The squatter bleeds straight away. The Policebot leaks oil. Shooting the crate puffs dust. Switch juice off and on to compare: the fight logic is identical both ways, and only the feedback changes.

Try it: space

Space follows the same rules, and repeater fire is a volley too. Repeater bolts first flare on the shield around the ship, at the spot where they land. When the shield's segments run out, it shatters, and later hits breach the hull with sparks, molten droplets, venting gas and debris. A damaged ship keeps venting. A missile trails smoke. A hit on the pilot cracks the canopy and spatters it with blood (Hegemony canopies stay dark). A kill freezes the game, shakes the screen and breaks the ship into three.

Explosions and other effects

The old Effects section now lives here. Explosions play in stages: a white flash, an ink-outlined fireball that cools from yellow to orange to red, a shockwave ring on the ground, then a rising smoke column, with embers, debris and a scorch mark from Particles(). Ships break into three spinning pieces when killed. Shields shatter into hexes. Damage numbers overshoot, arc up and fade; crits are bigger and shake. Loot pops out of the crate in a ring of sparkle. Promotions throw confetti.

Transitions

Screen changes are juice too. SR_ART.transition(ctx, W, H, k, type) covers the screen by k = 0.5 and reveals it again by 1, so the game swaps scenes while the screen is covered. There are four types: an iris into a mission, centred on the target; rebel stripes for tab and screen changes; hyperspace streaks between the galaxy map and space combat; and a flag sweep with a word for mission start or liberation.

Fire support

Fire support is drawn from the ground. Ships fly high, with their shadow cast on the ground below (flyover()). In a Strafing Run, the starfighter makes its pass while ten impacts walk up the ladder. With Door Gunner Support, the Graf circles with its door guns fitted and sweeps up to three targets. A Supply Drop comes down on a rebel-red parachute and lands as a crate you can open. For Reinforcements, the transport lands in a ring of dust. Every effect takes k from 0 to 1, so the game keeps control of the timing.

Facing

The world stays top-down and units face any angle, but Bobbleheads are drawn in three views: front, side (mirrored for west) and back. viewFor(u.face) converts the angle. The aim angle still drives the gun and the pupils exactly, so a unit facing south-east shows its face but points its gun at the real target.

Units and props share one depth sort by anchor y. Walls have a front face 26 units tall, so a unit standing right behind one would be hidden. When a unit's anchor is within 40 units north of a wall, that wall fades to 30%.

Terrain and props

Everything is drawn in 3/4 view: a top face and a front face, with the anchor at the centre of the footprint. Cover reads by height. Waist-high props are low cover, and anything taller than a head is high cover. The prop keys match PROPDEF in ground.js. Two biomes share one shape language: warm dust for the frontier, cool slate for Haven Rock.

Ships

Ships are built from parts: hulls, wings, fins, pods, cannons, turrets and engines. Each part takes a cel shade lit from the screen's top left, whatever the heading. On top go panel lines, rivets, vents, paint and markings. Livery is data, so one airframe can fly in several colours.

Revolution: scavenged and personal

Faded paint slapped on by hand, with drips and chips. Mismatched replacement panels with rivets, tape, scorch marks and old markings scratched out. A glass canopy with the pilot's face inside. Dustfall is a stolen Cross with its law star crossed out. The Talon still wears its racing number and checkers, with one wing salvaged from another airframe. Marta is a converted Graf hauler with welded plates, door guns and "Gran" nose art.

Hegemony: maintained and menacing

Clean white-and-navy or gunmetal hulls, glowing blue trim and the hex-eye emblem. Neat registry codes and no damage until combat. There are never faces: a dark visor slit, or a tinted canopy with a glowing bar. The Mote is drab and squat, with a scanning eye that sweeps as it flies. The Scimitar is dark, curved and sharp, with white leading edges and wing commander chevrons.

Weapons and attachments

Ship weapons and attachments are drawn at mount points on each hull, from the ship's loadout, so swapping them changes the picture. Defaults follow the doc. Every Tier 1 ship carries BLS-T Light Repeaters except the Graf, which comes unarmed. The Scimitar adds Missiles as its secondary. The Door Mounted Gun is an attachment that puts a gunner in each Graf door and enables Door Gunner Support. Hard points are game data and aren't shown. The plates and fuel tank below are placeholder examples until the attachment list exists.

Drones

AutoCom's drones fly in Hegemony Security grey and navy. Like all Hegemony craft they have no faces, just sensor eyes and visor slits. The Monitor is small and weak, sweeps a scanner along civilian routes, and pulses rings while it calls in reinforcements (calling:true). The Pursuer is the sleek day-to-day hunter, with twin repeaters. The Mag-Clamper is bigger and unarmed, with two magnet arms. While it clamps (clamp:[x,y]), the magnets flare and draw crackling field lines to the target.

Damage

damage runs from 0 to 1 and comes from hull lost. Above 0.2 scorch marks spread, above 0.45 a panel opens to show sparking wiring, and above 0.6 the ship trails smoke, throws sparks, its nav lights flicker, its engine sputters and the pilot panics in the canopy. This matters most for the Talon, whose frame "can't take very heavy hits."

Vehicles

Ground vehicles all float. Each has a shadow on the ground, glowing lift pads and a slow bob. They're drawn with the same trick as the walls: the footprint lies flat on the ground at any heading and is pushed up into a box, so the faces toward the camera show. A body, a cab or cargo box, and glass on the cab's sides give each one depth without needing art for every angle. Hegemony vehicles use the security palette: white or navy, blue-and-white light bars and faceless tinted glass.

The Frontier Floatin’ Truck takes attachments at named mounts: a gun in the bed, with an optional gunner Bobblehead manning it; welded side plates; a ram on the nose; a roof spotlight; and cargo crates. As with ships, the loadout decides what's drawn. The Riot Dispersal Cruiser's roof hatch opens and its turret rises as turretUp goes from 0 to 1, then the turret aims at turretAng.

Strider Mk I

Autoworks' friendly neighbourhood enforcement strider is a riot-control war machine wearing a friendly face. It has an armoured, riveted hull, a dozer plate with chevrons, loudspeaker horns, a gas launcher rack, autocannon claws on armoured arms, and short crab legs. There's no head: the face is a hologram beamed up from a projector on the hull, with scanlines and a flicker. It's friendly cyan for citizens. For dissenters, which in the game means once the area is alerted, it turns harsh white-blue and angry, the eye strip across the hull lights up and the spotlight flares. When the rebels hack it (u.hacked), the hologram goes rebel red, the siren stops and a red flag goes up on the antenna. When it's down, the projector sputters an ERR. Damage makes the hologram glitch and adds smoke and sparks. From behind you see the hologram faintly, reversed.

Haven Rock

The base stays isometric. Crew in room views are full Bobbleheads at s ≈ 0.95 in place of the stick figures. A thought bubble shows what each person's job is. Workers use the work pose, with sparks. The derelict hauler sits in the hangar cave at damage 0.5 until it's restored.

Ships in the hangar

Each Tier 1 ship has a small hand-built 3D model (SR_ART.hangarShip()), so it can be parked facing any direction (heading). The models use bevelled hull blocks, upright tail fins, round engine barrels, glass canopy domes and a sensor dish on the Graf. They're projected into the room's isometric view and stand on landing struts over their own shadow with the engines cold. Livery paint, stripes and names sit on the hull tops. Ships without a model yet, such as drones and capital ships, fall back to shipIso().

Galaxy map

The galaxy map is drawn like a board game. Planets are chunky outlined discs with a hard drop shadow, Veray gets a ring, and Hegemony-held systems carry a blue marker. Routes are dotted lines, and Haven Rock flies the rebel flag. Ships in transit travel the routes as small Bobblehead ships.

Handoff
