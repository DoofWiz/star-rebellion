Bobbleheads: world art style guide

The visual language for everything in Star Rebellion that isn't HUD: characters, kit, terrain, effects, ships, Haven Rock and the galaxy map. It sits alongside the HUD style guide, uses the same ink and faction colours, and is built the same way.

Every picture here is drawn live by sr-art.js, the module Claude Code will add to the game. What you see on this page is exactly what the game will draw.

Rules of the house

Faces carry state

Aiming, shaken, hacking or down should read off the face before any number. Every pose picks an expression.

Red is ours, blue is theirs

Every rebel wears a red scarf or armband. Every hostile has a blue visor, scarf, hatband or trim. Gold stays with the HUD.

Swap, don't redraw

A character is assembled each frame from slots: body, head, weapon, gear and pose. New kit is a data entry, never new art.

One ink, one light

Ink outlines everywhere: 2.6 on bodies, 2.2 on held things, 3 on terrain. Light comes from the top left, with one cel-shade tone.

Rebels make do

Rebel kit and ships are patched, faded, taped and personal. The Hegemony's are clean, white and navy, glowing and faceless.

A quiet world, loud people

Terrain is low-contrast and muted. Saturation is spent on characters, props you can use, and effects.

Palette

Faction colours come straight from the HUD kit (SR_THEME.C), so changing --sr-rebel recolours scarves, stripes and HUD together. The world palette lives in SR_ART.W and SR_ART.BIOME.

Shade is always the base colour darkened by 22% (shade(col,-.22)). Highlights are white at 25–55% opacity. Never shade with black at low opacity, because it muddies on dark terrain.

Construction

At scale 1, one unit is one CSS pixel at zoom 1, which makes a Bobblehead about the width of the old token. The anchor is the point between the feet: that's where the unit's map position, depth sort, selection ring and shadow all sit. Characters are drawn in back-to-front layers: feet, slung weapon, pack, body, vest, belt items, scarf, shoulder weapon, head, face, hair, helmet, then held weapon, hands and props.

Faces

Nine expressions cover every state. Pupils look toward the aim direction. The brows do most of the work, so keep them thick. Blinks happen every few seconds when idle or moving. Hegemony troopers never show a face: their two visor slits narrow, tilt or go round instead. Policebots show a screen, and because robots don't panic, they show ?! rather than sweat.

Cast and archetypes

Named crew have fixed genes in SR_ART.CAST, but their injuries, traits, rank and kit still show, like everyone else's. Every other rebel is generated (next section). Hostiles and bystanders come from an archetype in SR_ART.ARCH, chosen from the unit's existing flags.

Portraits

SR_ART.portrait() draws head and shoulders in a circle, for crew lists, recruit cards and the comms log. These replace the current initials.

Generated rebels

Most rebels aren't authored. They're generated by the rebel system (Rebel.gen), then shaped by everything that happens to them. SR_ART.lookOf(p) turns any rebel into a character. Nothing about their look is saved. Their face comes from a hash of p.id, so a rebel looks the same in every session, and everything else is read live from the rebel. Old saves need no migration.

A rebel's life shows

The same rebel across a career: recruited, blooded and promoted, injured, scarred, fitted with a prosthetic, decorated, commissioned, and finally made a Hero. Each stage is just a change to their record.

Dress-up bench

Armoury: Revolution Level 1

This is the Rev Level 1 kit from Gadgets, Gear and Gunships: the things a revolution with no factories can scavenge, steal, buy or craft. Every item has two drawings from the same code. One is item art for the Armoury tab, loot and pickups (SR_ART.item()). The other is the item in use on a character. Crafted and scavenged kit looks it, with tape, pipes, exposed cores and scuffed paint.

Manufacturers

Everything is sorted by manufacturer, so players know what to expect. Each manufacturer has a look. A ? after a maker's name means I've proposed it because the doc doesn't say. Maker TBC means it's open.

Poses

One table, SR_ART.POSES, maps every game state to an expression, a weapon hold, a body squash, footwork, a prop and an overlay. Orders that are being carried out show the order's pose. Otherwise the unit shows its current state.

Which pose wins

  1. down: u.down
  2. surrender: u.surr
  3. cower: civilians when u.cower
  4. panic: coolStateG(u)==='panic' once the area is alerted
  5. fire: the shooter in the current engagement beat
  6. The order being carried out (see the table)
  7. walk when moving, otherwise idle

Facing

The world stays top-down and units face any angle, but Bobbleheads are drawn in three views: front, side (mirrored for west) and back. viewFor(u.face) converts the angle. The aim angle still drives the gun and the pupils exactly, so a unit facing south-east shows its face but points its gun at the real target.

Units and props share one depth sort by anchor y. Walls have a front face 26 units tall, so a unit standing right behind one would be hidden. When a unit's anchor is within 40 units north of a wall, that wall fades to 30%.

Terrain and props

Everything is drawn in 3/4 view: a top face and a front face, with the anchor at the centre of the footprint. Cover reads by height. Waist-high props are low cover, and anything taller than a head is high cover. The prop keys match PROPDEF in ground.js. Two biomes share one shape language: warm dust for the frontier, cool slate for Haven Rock.

Effects

Effects are fat, outlined and short. Every effect takes a progress value k from 0 to 1, so the game keeps control of timing. Rebel plasma is red, Hegemony plasma is blue, and ballistic tracers are gold-white.

Ships

Ships are built from parts: hulls, wings, fins, pods, cannons, turrets and engines. Each part takes a cel shade lit from the screen's top left, whatever the heading. On top go panel lines, rivets, vents, paint and markings. Livery is data, so one airframe can fly in several colours.

Revolution: scavenged and personal

Faded paint slapped on by hand, with drips and chips. Mismatched replacement panels with rivets, tape, scorch marks and old markings scratched out. A glass canopy with the pilot's face inside. Dustfall is a stolen Cross with its law star crossed out. The Talon still wears its racing number and checkers, with one wing salvaged from another airframe. Marta is a converted Graf hauler with welded plates, door guns and "Gran" nose art.

Hegemony: maintained and menacing

Clean white-and-navy or gunmetal hulls, glowing blue trim and the hex-eye emblem. Neat registry codes and no damage until combat. There are never faces: a dark visor slit, or a tinted canopy with a glowing bar. The Mote is drab and squat, with a scanning eye that sweeps as it flies. The Scimitar is dark, curved and sharp, with white leading edges and wing commander chevrons.

Weapons and attachments

Ship weapons and attachments are drawn at mount points on each hull, from the ship's loadout, so swapping them changes the picture. Defaults follow the doc. Every Tier 1 ship carries BLS-T Light Repeaters except the Graf, which comes unarmed. The Scimitar adds Missiles as its secondary. The Door Mounted Gun is an attachment that puts a gunner in each Graf door and enables Door Gunner Support. Hard points are game data and aren't shown. The plates and fuel tank below are placeholder examples until the attachment list exists.

Drones

AutoCom's drones fly in Hegemony Security grey and navy. Like all Hegemony craft they have no faces, just sensor eyes and visor slits. The Monitor is small and weak, sweeps a scanner along civilian routes, and pulses rings while it calls in reinforcements (calling:true). The Pursuer is the sleek day-to-day hunter, with twin repeaters. The Mag-Clamper is bigger and unarmed, with two magnet arms. While it clamps (clamp:[x,y]), the magnets flare and draw crackling field lines to the target.

Damage

damage runs from 0 to 1 and comes from hull lost. Above 0.2 scorch marks spread, above 0.45 a panel opens to show sparking wiring, and above 0.6 the ship trails smoke, throws sparks, its nav lights flicker, its engine sputters and the pilot panics in the canopy. This matters most for the Talon, whose frame "can't take very heavy hits."

Vehicles

Ground vehicles all float. Each has a shadow on the ground, glowing lift pads and a slow bob. They're drawn with the same trick as the walls: the footprint lies flat on the ground at any heading and is pushed up into a box, so the faces toward the camera show. A body, a cab or cargo box, and glass on the cab's sides give each one depth without needing art for every angle. Hegemony vehicles use the security palette: white or navy, blue-and-white light bars and faceless tinted glass.

The Frontier Floatin’ Truck takes attachments at named mounts: a gun in the bed, with an optional gunner Bobblehead manning it; welded side plates; a ram on the nose; a roof spotlight; and cargo crates. As with ships, the loadout decides what's drawn. The Riot Dispersal Cruiser's roof hatch opens and its turret rises as turretUp goes from 0 to 1, then the turret aims at turretAng.

Strider Mk I

Autoworks' friendly neighbourhood enforcement strider has crab legs, its two autocannons as claws, and a face screen. The screen smiles at citizens and turns angry for dissenters, which in the game means once the area is alerted. When the rebels hack it (u.hacked), the screen goes rebel red, the siren stops and a red flag goes up on the antenna. When it's down, the screen shows ERR. Damage cracks the screen and adds smoke.

Haven Rock

The base stays isometric. Crew in room views are full Bobbleheads at s ≈ 0.95 in place of the stick figures. A thought bubble shows what each person's job is. Workers use the work pose, with sparks. The derelict hauler sits in the hangar cave at damage 0.5 until it's restored.

Galaxy map

The galaxy map is drawn like a board game. Planets are chunky outlined discs with a hard drop shadow, Veray gets a ring, and Hegemony-held systems carry a blue marker. Routes are dotted lines, and Haven Rock flies the rebel flag. Ships in transit travel the routes as small Bobblehead ships.

Handoff

Two files go to Claude Code with this guide. sr-art.js is the module that draws every picture on this page. ART-HANDOFF.md is the migration spec: where the module plugs into ground.js, space.js and base.js, how unit state maps to specs and poses, the migration order, and the acceptance checks.

As with the HUD, the guard rails are no gameplay changes, existing saves still load, and the autoplay bot still runs.
